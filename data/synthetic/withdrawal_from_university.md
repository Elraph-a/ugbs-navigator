### Withdrawal from the University

Withdrawal ends a student's studentship. Unlike deferment, it is not a pause: a student
who withdraws and later wishes to return must normally apply for admission again through
the usual admissions process.

### Voluntary withdrawal

1. Write a letter addressed to the Registrar, through your Head of Department, stating
   your intention to withdraw and the effective date.
2. Have the letter endorsed by your Head of Department.
3. Obtain clearance from the Library, your hall of residence, and the Finance Directorate.
   Outstanding books, accommodation charges and fee arrears must be settled first.
4. Submit the endorsed letter and clearance form to the Academic Affairs Directorate.
5. Collect written acknowledgement of the withdrawal for your records.

### Before you withdraw

Speak to your academic advisor or the Counselling Unit first. Deferment is often the more
appropriate route where the difficulty is temporary — illness, bereavement or a short-term
financial problem — because it preserves your place on the programme.

### Fees and refunds

Fees already paid for a completed semester are not refunded. Whether any part of a current
semester's fees can be refunded depends on how far into the semester the withdrawal takes
effect; the Finance Directorate decides this case by case. A student with arrears remains
liable for them after withdrawal, and transcripts are normally withheld until they are
cleared.

### Academic record after withdrawal

Courses completed before withdrawal remain on the academic record and appear on a
transcript. A withdrawal does not erase grades already earned, and does not by itself
constitute an adverse academic finding.
